I study how to evaluate AI systems used in high-stakes settings, so that people can tell when a model's output should not be trusted. My current work looks at how vision-language and large language models interpret crisis-related social media, and how their outputs compare with human annotations.

In my ISCRAM 2026 paper, adding a post's text to its image improved caption accuracy but also introduced text-driven errors. In my current manuscript on Hurricane Dorian tweets, five model versions reached similar overall agreement with historical labels, yet that agreement hid systematic differences: all five rated the three tweets labeled Critical as Low or Medium.

I am a senior in Computer Science at Brigham Young University, with minors in Mathematics and Physics, graduating April 2027. I have been a research assistant in Prof. Amanda Hughes's HCI research group since November 2023.

I am applying to PhD programs for Fall 2027 and will be at CSCW 2026 in Salt Lake City, October 10–14.

Research

  • LLM labeling of crisis tweets (manuscript in preparation). I compared five model versions from three families on 355 Hurricane Dorian tweets with 433 photos against historical TREC-IS labels. Priority agreement ranged from 39.2% to 50.1%, GPT-6 Sol assigned Medium to 77.2% of tweets, and all five rated the three Critical-labeled tweets as Low or Medium. These figures measure agreement with historical labels, not correctness.
  • Crisis image captioning (ISCRAM 2026). I compared Gemini 2.0 Flash, Qwen2.5-VL, and BLIP on 204 CrisisFACTS image-post pairs, with and without the post text. I designed the scoring rubric and error taxonomy and carried out the human coding (Krippendorff's α = 0.69). Adding post text raised Gemini's caption accuracy from 75.5% to 91.7%.
  • Text-to-SQL with retrieval and LoRA, with Prof. Porter Jenkins, Fall 2025 – Winter 2026. On Qwen3.5-9B with BM25 retrieval, Shadow-LoRA raised exact match from 19.47% to 30.49%.
